The plates

Every angle on the conversation.

Plate I

Risers

Topics

Topics ranked by how strongly they are growing, not by how big they are.

How it is read

Trend score is posts per step in the last quarter of the period divided by posts per step before it. The bar shows the z score of a Poisson test, so a big number means the growth is very unlikely to be chance.

Plate II

Climbers

Keywords

Single words and two word phrases with significant growth.

How it is read

Every word and phrase that appears at least 5 times gets the same test as the topics. Phrases that always appear together are shown once.

Plate III

Faders

Cooling

What the crowd is talking about less than before.

How it is read

The same test, run the other way: words whose recent count is well below their earlier rate.

Plate IV

Newcomers

First signs

Words that were almost absent, and now keep coming up.

How it is read

A term counts as new when it appears at least 4 times in the recent window and at most a tenth as often before. These are often the first sign of a fresh narrative.

Plate V

Spread

Platforms

Where each topic lives, and the platform where it was first heard.

How it is read

Each cell is the share of that topic's posts on that platform; brighter means more. The right column names the platform of the earliest post in the topic, a hint of where it began before spreading.

Plate VI

Hashtags

#tags

The tags people reach for, and how fast each is growing.

How it is read

Bar length is how many posts carry the tag. The value shows its trend score and the platform where it is used most.

Plate VII

Shift

Share of talk

Each topic's share of the conversation, first half against second half.

How it is read

Shares are worked out among the topics on screen. A positive change means the topic took a bigger slice of the talk in the second half.

Plate VIII

Forecast check

Honesty test

The last three steps are hidden, forecast from the earlier data alone, then compared with what really happened.

How it is read

The forecast uses Holt exponential smoothing with a damped trend, tuned separately for every topic. It is compared with a simple guess that repeats the last value. The range is plus or minus 1.64 standard deviations of past errors, about 90%.

How the radar works

Four steps, and a few things it will never do.

1

Clean

Lower case; links, mentions, numbers and filler words in English, Hindi, Hinglish and Spanish are dropped. A repeat by the same account counts once.

2

Count

Words, two word phrases and hashtags are counted in every time step. A half finished last step is left out.

3

Cluster

Terms that share posts merge into topics. Fast growing terms get a seat, so a small new topic is not missed.

4

Test

A Poisson test separates real growth from noise, bursts raise warnings, and a tuned forecast looks three steps ahead.
