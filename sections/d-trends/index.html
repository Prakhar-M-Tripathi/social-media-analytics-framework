Section D: Trend and Topic Detection

Finds the keywords, hashtags and topics people are talking about, ranks which ones are rising fastest using a statistical test, predicts where each topic is heading, and checks how accurate those predictions are.

Early warnings

Topics, keywords and hashtags whose latest complete time step is far above their normal level (burst score of 3 or more, meaning at least 3 standard deviations above average, and at least 5 posts).

1. Rising topics ranked

Posts are grouped into topics by the words they share. The trend score compares recent activity with earlier activity (above 1 means growing). Confidence comes from a statistical test that checks the growth is not just random noise.

2. How topics change over time

Posts per topic in each time step. Dotted lines are the forecast for the next three steps and the shaded band is the likely range. Click a name in the legend to hide or show it.

3. Top rising keywords

Words and two word phrases with the strongest statistically significant growth (minimum 5 mentions).

4. Fading keywords

Words and phrases people are talking about less than before.

5. Emerging terms

Terms that were absent or very rare in the first 75% of the period and appear repeatedly in the recent window. These are often the first sign of a new narrative.

6. Hashtags

Most used hashtags, their growth and where they are used most.

7. Topics across platforms

Share of each topic's posts on each platform (darker means more). "Started on" is the platform where the topic first appeared, which shows where a trend began before spreading.

8. Shifting discussions

Share of conversation each topic holds in the first half versus the second half of the period.

9. Forecast accuracy check

To test the predictions honestly, the last 3 time steps are hidden, the model forecasts them from the earlier data only, and the forecast is compared with what really happened. It is also compared with a simple guess (repeat the last value).

10. Topic details

Pick a topic to see its keywords, related topics, platforms, forecast and example posts.

How it works

  1. Clean: posts are lower cased; links, mentions, numbers and common filler words in English, Hindi, Hinglish and Spanish are removed. Hindi script is kept. The same text posted again by the same account is counted once, so one account repeating itself cannot fake a trend.
  2. Find: single words, two word phrases and hashtags are counted in every time step.
  3. Group into topics: frequent terms that appear in the same posts are merged (Jaccard similarity of their posts above 0.3). Each post is assigned to the topic it matches best, with rare words counting more than common words (IDF weighting). Coverage shows the share of posts that fit a topic.
  4. Rank: trend score = average posts per step in the recent window (last 25% of the period) ÷ average before it, with +1 smoothing. A Poisson z test checks whether the recent count is higher than the earlier rate would explain. Confidence is High when z ≥ 3, Medium when z ≥ 2, otherwise Low. A topic is Rising only when its trend score is at least 1.2 and z ≥ 2.
  5. Burst: how many standard deviations the latest complete step is above the earlier average. A burst of 3 or more raises an early warning.
  6. Predict: Holt's exponential smoothing with a damped trend forecasts the next 3 steps. Its smoothing weights are tuned separately for every topic to give the smallest past error, with a range of ±1.64 standard deviations of past errors (about 90%). Likely to rise means the forecast trend is upward and the growth is significant.
  7. Partial steps: if the newest time step is less than half complete, it is left out of the scores and forecasts so a half day does not look like a drop.

Input: data/posts.csv using the shared columns (post_id, platform, user_id, timestamp, text, parent_id). Until that file exists the page uses practice data: a larger built in Section D scenario (45 days, 6 platforms, with known events so the results can be checked) or Section A's demo file, chosen from the Dataset menu. Everything runs in your browser; no data is sent anywhere. Results are statistical estimates from word patterns, not certainties.
